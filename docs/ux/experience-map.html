Sitecraft Experience Map

Nine stages of the client journey, four lanes deep, with every point where the experience stalls or the commercial path goes missing. Built against the 52-check AEO rubric; the flow detail is a strawman to be red-lined.

Primary user Site owner Surface ReadTomato-hosted dashboard Goal 1 Boost content Goal 2 Attach RT services Gaps 12 client · 7 back office

The diagnostic is finished. The commerce is missing. The rubric stops exactly where the money starts. Class C — “content the site does not have” — is the product’s own machine-generated name for a content gap, itemised per page and already prioritised by severity. A content gap is precisely what Boost sells. Today it renders as an apology.

Everything below is downstream of that one inversion: the findings Sitecraft can’t fix are the product’s best sales asset, not its limitation.

The core inversion

One score hides the whole argument

A single number can only go up until it stops. Split into three bands, the same audit makes the case for Boost every time the client opens the page — without a single line of sales copy.

The hatched band is Class C: prices, credentials, hours, service areas, FAQs, pages that do not exist yet. It is the honest reason the score caps, and it is the Boost offer, sized in points the client already believes in. Illustrative figures.

Client-side gaps

Twelve places the experience leaks

Ordered by what they cost the Boost goal. Each is anchored to a rule in the rubric, not to a screen — so they hold even where my reconstruction of the flow is wrong.

Class C reads as a refusal, not an offer

F1 · critical
Today
“Flag only — never auto-edit.” Missing prices, credentials, hours, service areas and anything needing a page that doesn’t exist are reported, and the flow ends.
Cost
This is a per-page, severity-ranked list of content gaps generated for free by the audit — the Boost pitch, itemised. It currently presents as the thing Sitecraft can’t do.
Fix
Rename it “Needs new content.” Give every Class C finding a price and an estimated point gain, with one-click ordering. Bundle them — “Service-page facts pack · 6 pages · +14 pts.” The guardrail is untouched: Sitecraft still invents nothing, it sells the work of sourcing the fact.

One score, no visible ceiling

F2 · critical
Today
A single visibility number. Class A and B fixes move it; Class C findings hold points back invisibly.
Cost
The client applies everything available, plateaus, and reads the plateau as the product being finished. That is the exact moment they stop paying.
Fix
Three bands from the first scan: Fixed / Achievable now / Locked. Show the locked band on day one, before any purchase. It is a permanent, honest, zero-pressure Boost argument sitting on the dashboard.

No business-facts intake, so the locked band is bigger than it needs to be

F3 · critical
Today
Onboarding collects a URL. Because the guardrail correctly forbids invention, every unstated hour, price, service area and credential becomes Class C.
Cost
Much of “we can’t fix this” is really “nobody asked you.” The client is blocked by a question that takes them four minutes to answer.
Fix
A six-field Business Facts step at signup — legal name, service areas, hours, contact, credentials, price ranges or an explicit “not published” — plus the author registry (F11). Then show the client what they unlocked: “6 answers converted 9 blocked findings.” That single screen teaches the entire premise of Boost before you ever ask for money.

Nothing proves the AEO actually worked

F4 · critical
Today
Step 7 of the workflow re-parses the page: JSON-LD still valid, copy claims unchanged, no regressions. That is correctness.
Cost
The client bought visibility in answer engines and got back a passing lint. No felt win means no reason to buy more and nothing to renew on.
Fix
A citation check. Store 10–20 real buying-intent prompts per client — “best pediatric dentist in Ballard,” “Invisalign cost Seattle” — and run them monthly against the answer engines. Log whether the client is named, whether a competitor is, and the delta. This is simultaneously the retention engine, the Boost proof, and the one artifact a client forwards to their business partner.

Boost has no earned trigger moment

F9 · critical
Today
The goal exists. The moment doesn’t. An offer with no trigger becomes a nav item, and a nav item is wallpaper.
Cost
Pitched at signup it is noise, and it competes with the install you already need them to complete.
Fix
Three triggers only, in the order they earn the right — detailed in the next section. All three depend on F2 and F4 existing first.

Write access is demanded before any value is felt

F5 · important
Today
Signup and install sit between the teaser score and the full audit.
Cost
The hardest gate in the funnel sits in front of the aha. A dental office manager will not install a plugin on the practice site to see a list.
Fix
Audit before install. A read-only crawl needs no permission at all — deliver the entire 52-check audit, every page, on a free account. Ask for write access at the moment they click Approve on their first fix, where the ask explains itself.

The approval queue has no bulk path

F6 · important
Today
Class B is one diff, one decision. Twenty-six pages against 52 checks produces hundreds of them.
Cost
Abandonment mid-queue. The site ends half-fixed, the score half-moved, the product half-proven — and a half-proven product does not get a second purchase.
Fix
Group by check rather than by page: “Convert 14 label headings into claim headings”, scrollable diff set, one approve. Add trust escalation — after five approvals of a check type, offer to promote it to automatic for that account. Offer an “apply all safe fixes” on first run so session one ends with a visible jump.

Findings are ranked by severity, not by page value

F7 · important
Today
P0 → P1 → P2 → P3, Class A before Class B. Correct for risk.
Cost
Wrong for attention. Your own audit found the discipline stops at the blog’s edge — 8 of 26 pages instrumented, every commercial page bare. Client sites will look the same, so the queue fills with findings on pages nobody visits while the contact page waits mid-list.
Fix
Rank by severity × page value. Page value from analytics where connected, from nav depth and commercial intent where not. P0 stays absolutely first — deception is not negotiable — and everything below it sorts by what the page is worth.

Autonomous edits with no visible ledger

F8 · important
Today
Class A applies without asking to a live business website. The safety argument for that is sound and entirely invisible to the client.
Cost
One unexplained change on a medical page ends the account, whatever the change actually was.
Fix
A “What Sitecraft changed” ledger: every change, timestamp, check ID, before/after, one-click revert. Default new accounts to a seven-day dry run where Class A queues instead of applying, so the first week belongs entirely to the client. Weekly digest of what ran.

The reviews question has no compliant answer

F10 · important
Today
AggregateRating and Review are the rubric’s highest-risk rules and are hard-blocked, correctly.
Cost
The first thing a practice owner asks is why a competitor has stars in search and they don’t. “We never touch that” is the right policy and a terrible answer.
Fix
Give the refusal somewhere to go: connect the real review source, render genuine reviews on the page, then mark up only what is visibly there. Sitecraft still generates nothing. The connection is a natural paid add-on, and it retires your largest legal exposure by replacing a “no” with a path.

Author identity has no registry

F11 · important
Today
Correct Person schema per post, but the visible bio is whatever the CMS holds. Your own site carries one bio under six bylines, contradicting the structured data on five of them.
Cost
A machine-readable E-E-A-T failure — exactly the one the doctrine warns about — and it will be present on nearly every agency-built client site you onboard.
Fix
An author registry per account: name, role, bio, credentials, photo, stable @id. Reconcile every byline against it and flag mismatches as P1. A missing author becomes a Boost line item, not a dead end.

CMS drift is invisible

F12 · important
Today
Idempotency is guaranteed against a fixed page. It says nothing about a page the client’s theme update or page builder overwrites next Tuesday.
Cost
Silent regression. The score the client last saw quietly stops being true, and they hear about it from you never.
Fix
Scheduled re-crawl with drift detection. When the platform reverts a Sitecraft change, alert with the diff and a one-click reapply. It is also the best re-engagement email you will ever have, because it arrives with a real reason to log in.

Conversion architecture

Three moments to ask, and nowhere else

Boost is the primary goal, so it gets the earned moments. Everything else attaches to the shape of the findings rather than sitting in the navigation.

Trigger A · strongest available today

Verified score jump after first publish

Fires once, on the visit after the first Class A/B batch goes live and the re-scan confirms the delta. Momentum is at its peak and the client has just watched the product work.

“Your structure is clean now. What’s left is what these pages don’t say yet.”

Trigger B · always present

The locked band, on every visit

Not a prompt — a permanent element of the score display (F2). Ambient, honest, and it never wears out because it shrinks as the client buys.

“29 points are held by content that doesn’t exist yet. Here are the six pages holding them.”

Trigger C · strongest possible, doesn’t exist yet

A competitor named where they aren’t

Requires the citation check (F4). Per prompt, per page, with the competitor’s name in it. This is the most persuasive sentence this product will ever be able to write.

“Asked about implants in your city, ChatGPT named three practices. None of them was yours.”

Secondary services — attach to the finding, never to the nav

Web Reno — when P1 density per page exceeds what copy-bound fixes can move. The structure is the problem, not the words, and the audit can say so numerically.
Hot Take Engine / Instant Take — when the locked band is dominated by topics with no page at all, rather than facts missing from existing pages. That is a velocity problem, and it is a different sale from Boost.
Review connection — whenever the account has rating findings (F10). The only compliant answer to the most common question you will get.

Back office

Seven things a SaaS needs that a rubric doesn’t

The audit engine is genuinely well specified. The business around it is not yet modelled at all.

The billable unit isn’t defined

B1
Fix
Pages under management as the subscription unit — it is the thing that grows, the client understands it, and it meters the crawl cost honestly. Boost sells as à-la-carte content units. Citation-prompt count becomes the plan-tier lever, so the thing that proves value is also the thing that upgrades.

Class C has no operator inbox

B2
Fix
Flags addressed to “a human” with no human named will rot. Give them assignment, an SLA, and a state machine: open → info requested → converted to Boost order → declined. The conversion rate of that queue is the primary-goal metric.

Boost sales create delivery debt with no pipeline

B3
Fix
Order → brief → draft → risk review → client approval → publish → verify, as tracked states with owners. Build this in the same sprint as F1. Selling Boost without it turns the primary revenue goal into the primary support problem.

Risk review isn’t a workflow state

B4
Fix
Dental, medspa and senior-living claims need a human gate before publish. Today that is a sentence in a document. It should be a status that disables the publish control until a named reviewer clears it.

No finding-to-revenue attribution

B5
Fix
Stamp the originating check ID on every Boost order. Within a quarter you will know which of the 52 checks actually sells, and the audit can start leading with those.

Multi-location and agency accounts are unmodelled

B6
Fix
Roles, per-site billing, roll-up score reporting. Dental groups and realtor teams are the natural buyer for this product and they arrive with four to forty sites, not one.

No stated policy for lapsed accounts

B7
Fix
If a subscription ends, applied edits stay on the client’s site — they are improvements to property the client owns. Say that plainly at signup. It removes a real objection and costs you nothing, because the monitoring is what they were paying for.

Sequence

What to build first, and why in this order

Split the score and ask for the facts

F2 with F3. Together they make the argument that content is the lever, using nothing but the audit the client already ran — before a single offer appears. Cheapest to build, and every later trigger depends on the bands existing.

Turn Class C into an orderable offer, with fulfillment behind it

F1 with B3. This is the primary revenue goal and it needs both halves shipped together. The front half without the back half sells work you cannot deliver; the back half without the front never gets an order.

Ship the citation check

F4, which unlocks F9 Trigger C. It converts a lint report into evidence, gives renewal something to stand on, and produces the single most persuasive sentence available to this product. Everything else in the register is real, and sequenced behind these three.
